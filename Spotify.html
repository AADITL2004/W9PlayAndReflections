Feature 01 · Personalisation

made just for you ♡

What Spotify does
Home is shaped around what you listen to: personalised mixes and recommendation shelves (long-running examples include Discover Weekly and the Daily Mixes), plus Your Library, where your saved songs, albums and playlists live.
The emotional design principle
Personalise the experience, which links to exclusivity from my notes: the feeling that something was made for you.
Why it can work (HCI)
Relevant suggestions cut down the effort of finding something to play (behavioural level). A library that slowly fills with your choices builds a sense of ownership and personal relevance (reflective level). Together, that can turn into emotional connection and repeat engagement.
  • personal relevance
  • perceived ownership
  • emotional connection
  • less effort
  • engagement
my reflection

"I like how Spotify makes music feel personal rather than presenting everyone with exactly the same experience. The interface gives me a sense that my listening habits and preferences matter."

but also… recommendations can loop you back into the same few sounds, and a wrong guess can feel oddly impersonal. Personalisation only feels good when it feels accurate.

Feature 02 · Spotify Wrapped

your year, in your music ✨

What Spotify does
Toward the end of each year, Wrapped turns a listener's history into a short, tap-through story (top artists, songs and genres) with cards designed to be shared.
The emotional design principles
Storytelling, surprise and anticipation, plus prizes-and-surprises from my list of techniques.
Why it can work (HCI)
A table of play counts is information; a sequence of reveals is a story. Pacing the data one slide at a time makes each one a small surprise, and framing it as who you were this year moves it to the reflective level, tied to identity and memory. Share cards then add social connection, which makes the whole thing more memorable.
  • reflection
  • identity
  • surprise
  • anticipation
  • storytelling
  • shareability
  • social connection
  • memorability
my reflection

"Wrapped is a particularly interesting example because it gives people a reason to revisit their experiences and share a personal story. It makes listening history feel like something worth celebrating."

but also… not everyone wants their year summarised, or broadcast. A shared account or a sleep-sounds habit can produce a story that doesn't feel like you at all.

Feature 03 · Re-engagement

hey, we miss you ♡

What Spotify does
When a Premium subscription expires, a returning listener can see warm, personal messages that invite them to come back and reconsider their subscription.
The emotional design principles
Familiarity, a sense of continuity (your music is still here), personal relevance, anticipation, and loss aversion: people tend to weigh losing something more heavily than gaining something.
Why it can work (HCI)
It reminds people of value they've already built (playlists, a library, years of taste) instead of selling something new. That continuity can make returning feel natural rather than like starting over.

the critical bit: emotional design isn't automatically positive. The same warm message that feels friendly to one person can feel guilt-inducing, intrusive or manipulative to another. The line sits between an invitation to return and pressure to resubscribe.

an invitation ♡
  • says clearly what you're choosing
  • makes "no" as easy as "yes"
  • reminds you what you keep
  • respects your timing
emotional pressure ✕
  • guilt-trips ("don't leave us!")
  • hides the way out
  • threatens what you'll lose
  • repeats until you give in

Feature 04 · Musical identity

a soundtrack that feels like you ♪

What Spotify does
Listeners save albums, build personal playlists and collect music over time. That collection sits right next to the discovery features that keep adding new songs to it.
The emotional design principles
Personalisation, familiarity and ownership, plus the surprise of discovery.
Why it can work (HCI)
Music is closely tied to memory and mood, so revisiting familiar songs can bring back nostalgia and a sense of who you were. Discovery adds small moments of delight, and the habit of returning to "your" playlists builds engagement. For some people, though, it's simply background music, and that's fine too.
  • personalisation
  • familiarity
  • ownership
  • nostalgia
  • emotional connection
  • habit & engagement
my reflection

"I find it interesting that music collections can become more than a list of songs. They can reflect different moods, memories, relationships, and parts of someone's identity."

Back to my notes · the 3 cognitive levels

the three levels, but make it spotify

Visceral · first impressions

Visceral

Immediate reactions to the interface's colours, imagery, animations and visual composition: bold artwork, dark backgrounds, things that move with the music.

Behavioural · ease of use

Behavioural

How easily people can discover music, play songs, organise their library and finish what they came to do, often without thinking about it.

Reflective · after-use judgement

Reflective

How people interpret their listening history, memories, preferences and identity through their music. Wrapped lives here, mostly.

the levels overlap. Wrapped's colours are visceral, tapping through it is behavioural, and what it says about you is reflective. No feature belongs to just one level.

Back to my notes · hierarchy of user needs

from functional to delightful

A conceptual application of the pyramid from my notes. Spotify hasn't been formally evaluated against it here; it's a way of thinking about which layers the good feelings rest on.

Pleasurable

Pleasurable. Personalised recommendations, engaging visuals, Wrapped and emotionally meaningful moments can add delight beyond basic functionality.

Usable

Usable. People need to navigate music, find content and manage their libraries without unnecessary effort.

Reliable

Reliable. The experience depends on playback, saved content and core functions working consistently.

Functional

Functional. The app provides music playback and music discovery.

so, why does it work? ♡

Spotify shows that emotional design can go well beyond a nice-looking interface. Its strongest moments connect functionality with personal relevance, anticipation, identity, storytelling and social sharing, so the app feels less like a tool and more like something that knows you a little.

But good emotional design should respect people's autonomy and well-being, not just chase engagement or re-engagement. The warmest message is the one that's also easy to say no to.

good design works. great design makes you FEEL something.
